0A. The Condition and the Urgency
──────────────────────────────────

The condition is extensive. Its impact on shared spaces, shared meals,
and the ability to be fully present with others is significant enough
that standard behavioral approaches have a real ceiling. The nervous
system is operating at a chronically elevated baseline that predates
any given trigger event. Every therapeutic intervention attempted from
that baseline is working against a headwind.

The protocol therefore does not begin with behavioral work. It begins
with structure: first, lower the floor. Then rewrite what runs on it.


0B. What This Protocol Is
──────────────────────────

This is a personal healing and wellness protocol built for one specific
person, addressing one specific problem: misophonia. It is the product
of an extended inquiry into what neuroscience, behavioral research,
plant medicine, and evidence-based nutrition each have to offer a
condition for which conventional medicine has no established treatment.

The protocol integrates multiple domains not because complexity is a
virtue, but because misophonia is a multi-layered condition that
genuinely requires tools working at different levels simultaneously.
Each element has a specific mechanistic reason to be here. Each earns
its place.

The primary goal is clear: durable, lasting reduction in misophonic
reactivity — sufficient to no longer meaningfully impair daily life,
relationships, or the ability to share space with others. The wellness
work woven throughout is not a separate agenda. It builds and sustains
the same biological substrate that neuroplasticity runs on. A brain
that rewires most effectively is well-nourished, well-rested, and
operating from a calm physiological baseline. These things are
inseparable.

Success is measured by the A-MISO-S questionnaire, SUDs trajectory,
and most importantly, behavioral change in the avoidance inventory:
what situations are you no longer avoiding?

This protocol is a living document. It establishes a starting point
and a structure — not a rigid prescription. As the work progresses,
individual elements will be adjusted based on felt sense, observed
effects, bloodwork, and what the nervous system is actually responding
to. Some things will work better than expected and deserve more emphasis.
Some will need modification. Some may be deprioritized entirely. That is
not protocol failure — it is protocol intelligence. The structure here
is the best current map. The territory, as always, takes precedence.


0C. How Misophonia Works and Where This Protocol Intervenes
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

THE CORE CIRCUIT

When the misophonic brain hears a trigger sound, it involuntarily
activates the sensorimotor cortex corresponding to the body part
producing that sound — a mirror neuron-like process where the brain
simulates the action. That motor simulation cascades into the anterior
insula (threat signal), the anterior cingulate cortex (amplification),
and the autonomic nervous system (fight-or-flight response). This fires
faster than conscious regulation can intercept it.

When the trigger is associated with a specific person, a second layer
activates simultaneously: social threat coding — the sense that the
triggering person is doing something to you. And running ahead of
everything is anticipatory dread: the system pre-activates before any
sound occurs, keeping the cascade perpetually primed.

Three layers. Three different neurological substrates. Three different
sets of tools.

THE THREE TRIGGER LAYERS

Layer 1 — Acoustic-Sensory
The raw conditioned response to the sound itself.
Primary tools: counterconditioning, psilocybin, tVNS, SSP.

Layer 2 — Interpersonal-Violation
Social threat coding attached to specific people.
Primary tools: MDMA-assisted processing, EMDR, IFS.

Layer 3 — Anticipatory-Dread
Pre-exposure anxiety that keeps the system chronically pre-activated.
Primary tools: SGB, HRV biofeedback, Yoga Nidra, somatic release,
tVNS, kava.

The anticipatory layer is addressed first. Without reducing chronic
pre-activation, the acoustic layer cannot be worked effectively, and
the interpersonal layer cannot be safely approached at depth.

WHY STANDARD BEHAVIORAL THERAPY HAS A CEILING

The misophonic threat response fires because of a conditioned circuit
that activates faster than top-down regulation can reach it. Passive
exposure to that cascade does not correct it — in some cases it deepens
it. Counterconditioning is the operative mechanism: actively pairing
the trigger with a genuinely felt positive emotional state, building a
competing association strong enough to inhibit the threat response.
This requires a calm nervous system, genuine felt positive emotion,
sufficient neuroplasticity for the new association to write durably,
and sleep to consolidate it overnight. This is what the protocol is
designed to produce — those four conditions, simultaneously,
repeatedly, over a year.


0D. The Four Pillars and Governing Principles
───────────────────────────────────────────────

THE FOUR PILLARS

Pillar 1 — Plasticity Opening
Destabilizes the rigid misophonic circuit. Makes synapses malleable.
Creates conditions in which new learning writes more deeply.
Tools: Psilocybin, MDMA, Stellate Ganglion Block, microdosing.

Pillar 2 — Behavioral Rewriting
Writes new competing associations while the circuit is open.
This is the actual rewiring. Without this pillar, an open window
closes on empty.
Tools: Counterconditioning (six techniques), EMDR, propranolol-
assisted reconsolidation, IFS, ACT, cognitive reappraisal.

Pillar 3 — Consolidation
Cements structural changes during sleep. Sustains the vagal tone
and prefrontal strength that makes rewiring hold.
Tools: tVNS, tDCS, HRV biofeedback (HeartMath), Safe and Sound
Protocol, Yoga Nidra, sleep architecture.

Pillar 4 — Substrate
Sustains the biological conditions neuroplasticity runs on:
BDNF, NAD+, cellular repair, reduced baseline arousal.
Tools: Exercise, fasting, nutrition, supplementation, somatic
release, sleep hygiene, kava.

THE GOVERNING PRINCIPLES

The autonomic baseline determines the ceiling of all other work.
Until the floor is lowered, every intervention is limited by it.
This is why structural autonomic intervention comes first.

Counterconditioning is the primary rewriting mechanism. Treatment
here means two things simultaneously: writing new associations into
the circuit, and stopping daily life from continuously re-stamping
the old ones. Both are required. An open plasticity window that
closes on an unrewritten circuit achieves little. A circuit being
rewritten while the environment keeps reinforcing the original
pattern gains less than it should.

The substrate determines how much sticks. Sleep consolidates every
structural change. Neglect the substrate and gains erode between
sessions.

The protocol must never replicate the condition it is treating.
If the protocol ever starts to feel like a burden, that is a signal
to reduce intensity — not push through. See Section 3.

Flexibility is built in, not bolted on. The structure serves the
nervous system. Not the other way around.

Your own nervous system is the primary instrument. Every device
in this protocol exists to develop a biological capacity that
eventually makes the device unnecessary. As that capacity builds,
the device becomes optional. That is not protocol failure — it
is protocol success at a deeper level. The direction of travel
is always toward more trust in your own biology, less reliance
on external measurement. The healing intelligence is already
present. This protocol creates the conditions for it to operate.

1B. Phase 0 — Structural Intervention and Baseline Testing
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Two things happen before Phase 1 begins. One structural, one
diagnostic.

THE STRUCTURAL INTERVENTION — DSR SGB

A bilateral Stellate Ganglion Block using the Dual Sympathetic
Reset (DSR) protocol. Right stellate ganglion first, left
stellate ganglion 24–72 hours later.

The right side suppresses amygdala reactivity, HPA axis overdrive,
and sympathetic hyperactivation — the primary targets for
misophonia's hypervigilance profile. The left side restores
systemic autonomic balance and parasympathetic coordination.
Together: a bilateral reset of the sympathetic baseline.

Provider requirements: anesthesiologist or pain physician
experienced in SGB. Ultrasound guidance non-negotiable. Disclose
all supplements and substances in advance.

What SGB does: lowers the sympathetic floor through temporary
interruption of overactivated NGF signaling in the stellate
ganglion. Not sedation, not suppression — a durable reset that
can persist for months to years. The effect develops over 1–4
weeks.

Horner's syndrome — temporary drooping of the eyelid, pupil
constriction, and mild flushing on the injected side — is a
normal and expected post-procedure finding. It confirms the
block reached the stellate ganglion effectively. It resolves
within hours.

Post-SGB (Days 1–3): rest Day 1. From Day 2, notice ambient life:
what is resting body tension when no trigger is present? When a
trigger occurs, does it start from a lower place? How quickly does
the system recover? These observations calibrate Phase 1.

PRE-SGB SUPPLEMENT AND SUBSTANCE PROTOCOL

Pause 1 week before any SGB session:
— Krill oil
— Algae oil (triglyceride top-up)
— NAC
— Vitamin E (if supplementing)
— Resveratrol (Phase 5 only)
All of the above resume 48 hours after the procedure.

Pause 7 days before:
— Microdosing (psilocybin)

Space at least 72 hours between a psilocybin macrodose session
and any SGB procedure.

Continue normally:
— Magnesium (all forms), Lion's Mane, Bacopa, Creatine,
  NMN, B-complex, B12, D3/K2, Ashwagandha, Lithium Orotate,
  Phosphatidylserine, L. reuteri

Stop alcohol 7 days before.

THE DIAGNOSTIC BASELINE

Three categories of baseline data before Phase 1 begins:

Comprehensive bloodwork: metabolic panel, CBC, lipids with ApoB,
full thyroid with TPO antibodies, hormones (testosterone, DHEA-S,
cortisol), inflammation markers (hsCRP, homocysteine),
micronutrients (vitamin D, B12, ferritin, zinc, magnesium RBC),
blood glucose markers (fasting glucose, HbA1c, fasting insulin),
IGF-1.

HPA axis profile: salivary cortisol panel — diurnal rhythm across
four time points.

Cellular NAD+ level: actual cellular NAD+ status before
supplementation begins. Calibrates NMN dosing.

These numbers form the pre-protocol biological portrait, repeated
at Year 1 completion.

SECTION 3 — DAILY OPERATING MODES
================================================================================

The protocol does not run at the same intensity every day. Three
modes are defined. On any given day you are in one of them. The
mode determines what the day looks like — not willpower.


3A. Green — Resourced
──────────────────────

Run the full current-phase rhythm. Active rewiring is available
and pursued.

Indicators: slept reasonably well, body available, no rising dread,
CC feels approachable, resting body tension below 4/10.

If a plasticity window is open — post-macrodose, post-SGB — bring
full presence. Maximum productive work happens when the window is
open and the system is resourced simultaneously.


3B. Yellow — Frayed
────────────────────

A support day. Baseline and regulation practices only. No active
trigger work.

Indicators: poor sleep, rising baseline irritability, body tension
above 5/10, dread around CC, general frayedness.

Full baseline layer: supplements, sleep protocol, meeting support.
Full support layer: Yoga Nidra, somatic release, Qi-Gong, resonance
frequency breathing.
Exercise if restorative. No CC. No hierarchy work. No deliberate
trigger exposure. Eat well. Rest deliberately.

One Yellow day requires no adjustment to the overall phase.


3C. Red — Tactical Brake
─────────────────────────

When warning signs accumulate, the protocol enters the Tactical
Brake. This is not a break from the protocol. It is a protocol
feature — the mechanism that prevents the work from compounding
the condition it is treating.

Activate if three or more are present, or any single one is severe:
— Sleep poor 3+ consecutive nights despite sleep protocol
— Baseline reactivity rising week over week
— CC sessions consistently ending higher than they began
— Growing dread or avoidance around CC itself
— Rumination increasing rather than decreasing
— Body tension baseline rising rather than falling
— Resentment toward the protocol has appeared
— Overall felt sense of the protocol is oppressive

Duration: minimum 48 hours. Extend until the system settles.

Active rewiring fully suspended: no CC, no hierarchy work, no
deliberate trigger exposure, no macrodosing or MDMA, no
propranolol, no EMDR.

Support layer doubled: Yoga Nidra nightly (40+ min), somatic
release every morning, Qi-Gong daily, resonance frequency
breathing daily. Exercise only if genuinely restorative.
Sleep protection maximized.

Baseline layer maintained: all supplements, all sleep protections,
no changes to nutrition or fasting.

Additionally: gentle movement in nature, reduce optional
commitments, do not use this time for protocol planning or analysis.

Return when: sleep stable 2+ nights, tension and dread clearly
lower, felt sense closer to "I could do a gentle session" than
"I dread it." First session back: 20 minutes, SUDs 1–3.

If the Brake is needed more than once in a phase, revisit the
phase gate. Pushing through warning signs produces sensitization
— and sensitization undoes previous gains.

4B. Meeting Support
────────────────────

Begins: Week 1, Day 1
Device: bone conduction headphones (Shokz OpenRun Pro 2)

When earplugs or noise cancellation create near-silence, the
brain's thalamic reticular nucleus increases its gain to search
for sound — amplifying the next trigger when it arrives. Bone
conduction headphones leave the ear canals open while playing
pink noise at barely perceptible volume through the cheekbones.
Speech is heard clearly and naturally. The pink noise keeps the
thalamic scanning reflex occupied and prevents gain increase.

Volume: barely audible — loud enough to register, forgotten
within 30 seconds.

Use at: morning meeting, leadership meeting, council meeting,
shared meals, any context where triggers are likely.

The existing earplug practice remains valid as backup. As CC
progresses, reliance on meeting support will reduce naturally —
by choice, not schedule.


4C. Sleep Protocol
───────────────────

Begins: Week 1. Runs throughout all phases.

Sleep is the consolidation mechanism. Every structural change
produced by CC, every dendritic spine grown by psilocybin, every
extinction association formed — all are cemented during sleep,
or partially dissolve without it.

Target: 7.5–8.75 hours nightly. Consistent fixed wake time.
Sleep onset target: 9:00–10:00pm.

EVENING WIND-DOWN (sequence — timing adapts to the day)

Sleep stack (see 4J)
Warm shower — lowers core body temperature, the primary
  physiological trigger for sleep onset
Yoga Nidra — 20-minute sleep track standard nights;
  35–45-minute open-awareness track when intensive work is running
Screens off
In bed: pink noise on, blackout mask on

MOUTH TAPING

Worth trialing for 1–2 weeks. Apply a small strip of soft medical
paper tape (3M Micropore or equivalent) vertically over the center
of the lips. If it improves sleep depth or reduces night waking,
continue. If it produces discomfort or anxiety, discontinue.

Nasal breathing activates parasympathetic tone, produces nitric
oxide, and may reduce the cortisol-driven arousals associated with
night waking.

BLACKOUT MASK

Any light penetrating the eyelids suppresses melatonin and reduces
sleep depth. A well-fitting blackout mask is non-negotiable.

PINK NOISE MACHINE

Pink noise enhances slow-wave sleep and next-morning recall —
mechanistically appropriate for a protocol actively writing new
associations that must consolidate overnight.

NIGHT WAKING

Do not check phone. Do not turn on light. Put on the 20-minute
sleep-specific Yoga Nidra track. This maintains the theta-alpha
state even if sleep does not return, preventing the cortisol-driven
frustration loop that raises the following day's reactive baseline.

MORNING

Fixed wake time. Bright light within 10 minutes — outside preferred.
Cold finish on morning shower: 30 seconds cold water. This anchors
the diurnal cortisol rhythm.

WHAT DISRUPTS SLEEP IN THIS PROTOCOL

Psilocybin: always morning sessions so effects resolve before sleep.
MDMA: disrupts REM 1–3 nights after — expected; maintain Yoga Nidra.
tDCS: not within 5 hours of sleep time.
Skipping Yoga Nidra: the most common reason the night-waking
  pattern returns.
Late meals: last real meal by 2:45pm. Optional closer by 4:15pm
  only if genuinely underfueled. Nothing after 4:15pm.


4D. Morning Practices
──────────────────────

These are not tasks to complete. They are the biology being
developed. The sequence is the anchor — the clock is not.

SOMATIC RELEASE — SIMPLIFIED VERSION (begins Week 3)

Chronic misophonic hypervigilance creates sustained defensive
bracing: shoulders elevated, jaw clenched, psoas contracted,
breath shallow. This muscular tension signals ongoing danger to
the brainstem, keeping the nervous system in anticipatory alert
regardless of whether any trigger is present.

Simplified version (Phase 1):
Constructive rest position: lie on back, knees bent, feet flat,
hands on lower belly. 5 minutes. Allow lower back to release
toward floor. Breathe into the hands.
Diaphragmatic reset: 2 minutes slow belly breathing — inhale
4 counts, belly rises, exhale 6 counts. Allow jaw and shoulders
to release.

7–10 minutes total. Run every morning before the day's demands begin.
Full instructions in Section 10.

QI-GONG (begins Week 4)

Daily, 15 minutes, morning. Two components: a shaking practice
(3–5 min, gentle bouncing from the knees to break overnight
somatic bracing) followed by a slow flowing practice (10 min,
intentional arm and torso movement). Full instructions in
Section 12.

WATERFALL RUN

This is your primary neuroplasticity platform. No device in this
protocol produces the breadth of simultaneous biological effect
that this does: BDNF elevation from Zone 2 exercise, bilateral
sensory integration through full-body movement, vagal activation
through cold immersion, cortisol rhythm anchoring, somatic reset.
These are all biological systems being developed directly.

Zone 2 climb + cold plunge 3–5 minutes (50 minutes total).
When unavailable: any Zone 2 effort 45 minutes + cold shower
30–60 seconds. The session matters more than the venue.

Target: two per week. Non-negotiable when possible.

RESONANCE FREQUENCY BREATHING

Six breaths per minute — 5-second inhale, 5-second exhale.
Simultaneously recall a genuinely felt positive emotion — physically
felt in the body, not conceptually invoked. The breathing alone is
insufficient. The felt emotion is what produces coherence.

This is the biological target of HeartMath biofeedback. The practice
can run independently of any device, anywhere, at any time. It is
the most portable regulation tool in the protocol.

YOGA NIDRA — EVENING (begins Week 1)

Introduced in Week 1 alongside the sleep protocol. The one practice
that should never be skipped even when everything else is missed.
Full protocol in Section 11.

MATCHA AND GREEN TEA

Matcha: morning, with or after first meal. Provides 300–400mg
L-theanine per cup — more than supplement capsules — plus EGCG
and moderate caffeine paired with theanine for calm alertness.

Green tea (kukicha or sencha): afternoon, 2–3 hours before sleep.
Lower caffeine than matcha. A ritual-quality afternoon drink.

Note: EGCG from both inhibits non-heme iron absorption. Space
both drinks at least 1 hour from iron-rich meals.


4E. HRV Tracking
─────────────────

Begins: Day 1 of Phase 1
Purpose: establish baseline, then confirm trajectory

The goal of HRV tracking in Phase 1 is to understand your
baseline — what your nervous system looks like before the
protocol's substrate work changes it. Once that baseline is
known (typically 6–8 weeks), tracking shifts from continuous to
periodic. A weekly or monthly check confirms trajectory. The
data confirms what felt sense observes. It does not replace it.

Choose the option with least friction that will actually be used:

Option A — Wearable with sleep tracking: continuous data.
Trial for 6–8 weeks to establish baseline, then use weekly
spot-checks unless a specific question warrants more detail.
If sleeping with a device meaningfully disrupts sleep, switch
to Option B — the data is not worth the disruption.

Option B — Morning chest strap reading: Polar H10 or equivalent,
5 minutes each morning, connected to HRV4Training or Elite HRV.

Option C — Weekly spot-check: fingertip pulse oximeter with HRV
function, same time and conditions each week. Sufficient to track
meaningful trends over months.

The least friction option that will actually be used consistently
is the right choice. By Phase 3, felt sense has become the
primary instrument. Data confirms or questions it.


4F. HeartMath HRV Biofeedback
──────────────────────────────

Begins: Month 2
Device: HeartMath Inner Balance (ear clip + app)
Mode: always Free Session (Quick Coherence) — never guided sessions

HeartMath is a learning tool, not a permanent fixture. Its purpose
is to teach you the felt sense of cardiac coherence — what it
actually feels like in the body when the shift into coherence
occurs. Once you can reliably feel that shift without the device,
resonance frequency breathing becomes the primary practice and
HeartMath becomes an occasional check-in.

Use HeartMath daily for the first 4–6 weeks of Month 2. Learn

as the daily practice, HeartMath as a weekly or less frequent
confirmation. This is the direction of travel — toward the
biology, away from the readout.

Protocol: breathe at resonance frequency (6 breaths per minute —
5-second inhale, 5-second exhale). Simultaneously recall a
genuinely felt positive emotion. The breathing alone is
insufficient. The felt emotion is what produces coherence.
Give this practice full attention.

App note: always Free Session mode — no narrator. The guided audio

use the guided sessions. Always run through headphones.

Avoid: distracted sessions — they produce nothing. Not on

genuinely dissociated.


4G. Safe and Sound Protocol — SSP
───────────────────────────────────

Begins: Month 2, Weeks 3–4
Platform: Unyte-iLs (licensed provider required)
Total: 5 hours — 30–60 minutes per session over 1–2 weeks

The SSP uses music with the human vocal frequency range
acoustically enhanced to retrain the neural circuits governing
auditory safety detection. The stapedius muscle in the middle ear,
when well-regulated, dampens low-frequency (threat-associated)
sounds and enhances the vocal (safety-associated) range. In chronic
hypervigilance the stapedius is undertrained. SSP exercises it
upstream of the conditioned trigger response — at the auditory
hardware level before the threat cascade fires.

SSP does not do what CC does. It addresses what CC cannot reach
from above. The two are complementary.

Running SSP: lie down or sit comfortably. Headphones — not speakers.
No multitasking. If emotional release, fatigue, or hypersensitivity
occurs: slow down, do not push through.

On SSP days: do not run standalone CC sessions.

Primary course: use the licensed provider version in Phase 1 — this
is the validated intervention and the foundation everything else
builds on. The Phase 4 refresher can use daily listening to vocal
music in the human vocal frequency range (acoustic folk, unprocessed
vocal harmonies, classical violin) for 20–30 minutes in a mildly
alert state. This captures most of the mechanism without requiring
another provider course.

After primary course: 2–3 days before pushing CC hierarchy.

ON OMEGA-3 SOURCES

This protocol's neuroplasticity targets require sustained high
levels of EPA and DHA in phospholipid form for neuronal membrane
incorporation. DHA is the dominant fatty acid in the brain's gray
matter and myelin. During intensive neuroplasticity work, neuronal
membranes are being actively remodeled — the availability of DHA
in phospholipid form directly affects how efficiently new synaptic
connections form and stabilize.

Algae oil provides EPA and DHA in triglyceride form, well absorbed
and raises blood levels effectively. Krill oil provides them in
phospholipid form, which crosses into neural tissue more efficiently.
There is no plant-based phospholipid EPA+DHA source available in
supplement form — krill oil is the only practical source of this
delivery mechanism.

Oily fish (sardines, mackerel, salmon) provides EPA+DHA at
~1500–2000mg per 100g. Fish is used occasionally rather than as
a staple — algae oil carries this load daily. On fish days: skip
both krill and algae oil supplements. On mackerel days: also skip
or halve phosphatidylserine — mackerel provides ~480mg PS per 100g
(see Section 14F).

SECTION 5 — COMMUNITY-LIFE CONTAINMENT DURING REWIRING
================================================================================

The formal CC session is the engine of behavioral rewriting. But
the nervous system does not distinguish between a CC session and
daily life. Every communal meal, every morning meeting, every car
ride is also a data point — either reinforcing the old circuit or
failing to add to it. The goal during active rewiring phases is
not to eliminate real-world exposure, but to ensure that real-world
encounters do not continuously re-stamp the original pattern faster
than CC is rewriting it.


5A. Meals
──────────

Pre-entry check: run the Green/Yellow/Red assessment before
entering any shared meal. On Yellow days, apply full containment
protocol. On Red days, a solo meal is not avoidance — it is
appropriate protection.

Seating: edge or corner seating with clear exit access. Back to
a wall where possible. This reduces the number of people in the
peripheral sound field and preserves the option to leave without
social friction.

Sound support: bone conduction pink noise as default. Earplugs
as backup for high-load days.

Meal duration: there is no obligation to stay for the full duration.
A short, complete meal followed by a clean exit is a regulated
experience. Forcing a prolonged stay when the nervous system is
escalating is reinforcement, not practice.


5B. Morning Meetings and Council
────────────────────────────────────

Pre-entry: same Green/Yellow/Red check. On Yellow, full
containment. On Red, attendance is optional.

Sound support: bone conduction pink noise as default from Week 1.

Physical position: aisle or exit-adjacent seating where the
format allows.

Stepping out: brief exits require no explanation.

Post-meeting decompression: 3–5 minutes before transitioning
to the next demand. Physiological sigh, slow walk outside,
or simply standing in a quiet space and letting the nervous
system settle.


5C. Car Rides
──────────────

One-on-one rides with a person whose sounds are high on the
trigger hierarchy carry significant risk — enclosed space,
sustained proximity, no exit. Group rides distribute the risk.

Mitigation in high-risk rides:
— Window open, even slightly — adds ambient noise
— Music or podcast playing as background
— Front seat when possible
— Bone conduction headphones are not conspicuous in a car

When declining is appropriate: if the ride is non-essential,
one-on-one, and the nervous system is already Yellow.

When accepting with containment is better: if declining would
create more social friction than the ride itself.


5D. Yoga and Shared Quiet Practices
───────────────────────────────────────

Structural silence heightens thalamic scanning. This is not a
reason to avoid yoga — it is a reason to approach it strategically.

Position: edge placement, near the door, not surrounded.

Sound: bone conduction at barely perceptible volume during setup,
then off once practice begins and the system is settled.

If escalation occurs mid-practice: leave quietly. The practice
is not a CC session. Staying through flooding advances nothing.


5E. Work Coordination and One-on-One Interactions
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Walking meetings: a genuine tool. Side-by-side movement reduces
the face-to-face acoustic intimacy of sitting conversations.

Short check-ins over long discussions: preference for brief,
task-focused exchanges with people whose sounds are high on
the trigger hierarchy.

Written communication: using text or written formats for non-urgent
exchanges is not social withdrawal — it is a practical tool during
active rewiring phases.


5F. Post-Event Reset
───────────────────────

After any high-load communal context — regardless of how well
it went — a brief reset before the next demand:

2–5 minutes. Options:
— Physiological sigh (double inhale through nose, long exhale
  through mouth) × 3
— Brief walk outside, no destination
— Constructive rest position, eyes closed
— Cold water on face and wrists
— Three slow breaths with jaw deliberately released

The reset is not analysis time. It is a physiological action.
Two minutes. Then continue.

The distinction that matters: "I got hit" versus "I am spiraling."
The reset interrupts both. The anti-rumination protocol in
Section 6E is the next step if spiral is present after the reset.


5G. In a Triggered Moment — The Three Moves
─────────────────────────────────────────────

When the trigger fires and fight-or-flight is active, cognition
is offline. Any instruction that begins with "notice" or "reframe"
will fail. The body moves first.

Move 1 — Physiological (first 5 seconds):
One physiological sigh. Double inhale through the nose — two
sharp inhales stacking — then one long slow exhale through the
mouth. This mechanically activates the vagal brake. Hardware,
not philosophy.

Move 2 — Orientation (10 seconds):
Feel your feet on the floor. Feel the weight of your body.
One thing you can see. This tells the brainstem: I am here,
I am not in danger of dying, this is a room.

Move 3 — Listening (10–20 seconds):
Instead of the habitual move — contract, brace, escape — a
moment of genuine curiosity about what is happening inside you.
Not about the sound. About your own interior.
Where is it in your body? What shape does it have?
What is the quality of the sensation before the story starts?

This is not asking you to like the sound. It is asking you to
be more interested in your own experience than in the threat.
That shift — from what they are doing to me to what is
happening in me — is the beginning of a different relationship
with the condition.

If the system is genuinely flooded after these three moves:
leave. A regulated exit is always the right call. The listening
happens in the window before flooding, not after it.

The entire sequence is under one minute. Most of it is silence.

6A. Counterconditioning — How It Works
────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT CC IS AND IS NOT

CC is not exposure therapy. Exposure therapy relies on habituation
— repeat until the nervous system stops reacting. The misophonic
threat circuit fires faster than top-down regulation can reach it.
Passive exposure does not correct this. It can deepen it.

CC actively pairs the trigger with a genuinely felt positive
emotional state, building a competing association strong enough
to inhibit the threat response. Two non-negotiable conditions:
1. The positive state must be physically felt in the body — not
   conceptually invoked.
2. Entry condition must be met: SUDs 0–2, body tension below 4/10.
   If this cannot be reached after 5 minutes of resonance frequency
   breathing, the session does not begin. This is a Yellow day.

PAIRED STIMULI LIBRARY — BUILD BEFORE FIRST SESSION

Build two playlists before starting CC:

POSITIVE STIMULI (15–20 items):
Music, nature sounds, or sensory experiences that reliably produce
felt positive emotion in the body. Test each: does it produce an
actual physical shift — warmth, expansion, relaxation? If not,
it does not qualify. Build from your own history: music from
meaningful times, sounds from places that feel safe, pieces
that reliably land in the body.

TRIGGER SOUND LIBRARY:
Validated sounds: FOAMS database (Zenodo, 32 files across 8
sound classes) for SUDs 1–6 range. Freesound.org and yt-dlp
(YouTube extraction) for additional sourcing. Self-recording for
higher-hierarchy and person-specific sounds.

Sounds organized by hierarchy level. Know the SUDs rating of each
before any session. Never introduce a sound above SUDs 4 in Phase 2.

BASIC TECHNIQUE — POSITIVE PRIMING + TRIGGER INTRODUCTION

1. Somatic release sequence (see 4D / Section 10)
2. Resonance frequency breathing — 3–5 minutes until genuinely
   calm. SUDs 0–2 confirmed.
3. Begin positive stimuli — music or nature sounds. 2–3 minutes.
   Wait for the positive state to be felt in the body, not just
   recognized mentally.
4. Introduce trigger sound at low volume. Positive music dominant.
   Hold for 2–4 minutes. Positive state must remain felt.
5. Remove trigger. Return fully to positive stimuli. Let the
   positive state rebuild completely.
6. Repeat 4–6 times per session. Stop if SUDs rise above 4 or
   positive state cannot be recovered.

Full CC technique library in Section 13.

SESSION FREQUENCY

2–3 sessions per week. A session entered from genuine stillness
does more than two sessions entered because the schedule says so.
The entry condition governs — not the calendar.

On a day the entry condition cannot be reached after 5 minutes
of resonance frequency breathing: this is a Yellow day. No session.


6B. tVNS (Nuropod) During CC
──────────────────────────────

Begins: Phase 2. Use during CC sessions.

Vagus nerve stimulation during extinction learning accelerates
the rewriting. The Nuropod clips to the tragus of the left ear
and delivers gentle electrical pulses through the auricular branch
of the vagus nerve, activating the parasympathetic system during
the session.

This is a precision tool, not a session requirement. Before each
session, assess: will having the device clipped enhance the
quality of the session without adding distraction? If yes, use it.
If managing the device would interrupt the session's quality,
omit it.

As resonance frequency breathing develops your own vagal
activation capacity, tVNS becomes optional support rather than
required infrastructure. The goal is to not need it. When you
can maintain felt positive state and regulated baseline during
a CC session through breath and body alone, the device has
served its purpose.

Avoid: during driving or activities requiring full alertness.
Not during macrodose or MDMA sessions.

6D. ACT — Acceptance and Commitment Therapy
─────────────────────────────────────────────

Begins: Phase 2
Resource: The Happiness Trap (Russ Harris)

ACT addresses the secondary suffering layer: distress about having
the distress, self-criticism for reacting, anticipatory anxiety,
and rumination between CC sessions. It does not reduce the trigger
response — CC does that. It makes the period before CC completes
its work livable.

Defusion practice (daily, 2–3 minutes): when any trigger-related
thought, self-criticism, or anticipatory worry arises, prefix it
silently: "I notice I am having the thought that..."
This creates distance between the thought and the thinker without
suppressing content. The more automatic it becomes, the more
useful it is.

Values-based action (weekly, one instance): one action consistent
with who you want to be that requires tolerating some misophonic
discomfort. Note that you did it.


6E. Anti-Rumination Protocol
──────────────────────────────

Anticipatory and post-event loops can consume more nervous system
bandwidth than the actual triggers. Left unaddressed, rumination
keeps the threat circuit primed continuously.

PATTERN INTERRUPT — immediate
When rumination is recognized: physical break in the pattern.
Stand up, change room, cold water on face, 10-second shaking
practice, or the physiological sigh double-inhale. Break the
pattern first.

ACT DEFUSION — within 60 seconds
After the pattern interrupt: "I notice I am rehearsing the
scenario where..." Label it and let it be.

TIME CONTAINER — for genuine processing
If the loop contains something real — an actual unresolved
relational dynamic — give it 5–10 minutes, pen and paper,
then close the notebook. The container prevents the processing
from leaking across the whole day.

Rumination increasing rather than decreasing is a Tactical
Brake indicator. See Section 3C.


6F. Kava — Pre-Exposure Anxiolytic
────────────────────────────────────

Begins: Phase 2. Maximum 3x per week, situational.
Timing: 45–60 minutes before a high-trigger community context
or a CC session at a more challenging hierarchy level.

Traditional Pacific Islander anxiolytic with GABAergic and
kavalactone-mediated effects. Unlike alcohol, does not impair
cognition, disrupt sleep, or produce next-day effects. Reduces
the anticipatory-dread layer before high-trigger contexts.

Noble kava only — not instant, not extract, not tudei kava.
2–4 tablespoons medium-grind noble kava root powder in a strainer
bag. 2 cups room-temperature water. Knead and squeeze 5–10 minutes
until muddy brown. Drink the full preparation. Effects begin
15–30 min.

As CC progresses and the anticipatory layer reduces, kava's role
naturally decreases.

Avoid: macrodose days, MDMA days, with alcohol, daily use.

7A. tDCS — Transcranial Direct Current Stimulation
────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Begins: Phase 3
Device: ActivaDose tDCS
Timing: 20 minutes immediately before each CC session

tDCS is a precision instrument. It upregulates excitability of
the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC) — the region
governing top-down emotional regulation. Stronger left dlPFC
activation correlates with faster fear extinction and better
override of automatic threat responses.

Use tDCS before CC sessions on the days you run CC. It is not
a daily obligation and does not need to run on non-CC days.
The excitability window is active for approximately 30–60 minutes
post-stimulation — begin CC immediately when tDCS completes.

Placement:
Anodal electrode: left dlPFC — position F3 (10-20 EEG system),
above-left of center, roughly above the left eyebrow and back
2 inches.
Reference electrode: right supraorbital (above right eyebrow).

Settings: 1.5–2.0 mA. Begin at 1.5 mA. Brief tingling at onset
is normal and resolves within 60–90 seconds.

Avoid: not within 5 hours of sleep time. Not within 24 hours of
a macrodose or MDMA session. Not during Tactical Brake. Stop if
persistent headache develops.

7C. EMDR — Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Begins: Phase 3
Frequency: 2–3 sessions per week, post-CC session, on CC days
Target: specific interpersonal trigger memories — specific
incidents, specific people, specific moments. Not generic sounds.

EMDR activates a distressing memory while engaging bilateral
sensory stimulation (bilateral audio tones). The bilateral
stimulation appears to activate the brain's innate memory
processing mechanism — the same one operating during REM sleep
— allowing the memory to reprocess with reduced emotional charge.

For misophonia, EMDR targets the interpersonal-violation layer —
memories where the trigger is not just acoustic but relational.
Standard CC does not reach these memories directly. EMDR does.

Self-directed protocol: use a bilateral audio app (alternating
left-right tones at 1 Hz). Headphones required.

Step 1 — Target: one specific memory. Rate distress (SUDs 0–10).
Step 2 — Activation: bring memory vividly to mind. Hold it.
Step 3 — Bilateral stimulation: start audio. Hold the memory
while tones run. ~30-second sets. Notice what shifts.
Step 4 — Check-in: after each set, breath, note what came up,
re-rate distress. If reducing, run another set. If stable or
rising after 3–4 sets, close and ground.
Step 5 — Closure: when distress reaches 0–2, install a positive
cognition. Run 1–2 sets on this statement.
Grounding: 5 breaths, 5 things noticed in the room, feet on
floor. Do not end with distress above 3/10.

Working with a trained EMDR therapist is preferable for high-
charge memories. Self-directed EMDR is appropriate for SUDs 4–7.

Avoid: same day as a macrodose or MDMA session. During Tactical
Brake. When baseline distress is already elevated.


7D. IFS — Internal Family Systems
────────────────────────────────────

Begins: Phase 3
Frequency: 1–2 sessions per week, post-CC or standalone
Target: the protector parts managing the misophonic response

IFS works with the internal system that organizes around
misophonia: the protector parts that generate rage, dread, and
hypervigilance, and the exiled parts those protectors are
managing. These are not problems to be solved — they are
intelligences to be understood. The work is contact, not
eradication.

Self-directed entry point: sit quietly. Notice where misophonia
lives in the body. Ask that sensation: what are you protecting?
What are you afraid would happen if you weren't here? Listen
without demanding answers. The first session that produces
genuine contact with a protector part — not resolution, contact
— is a Phase 3 success.

Resource: No Bad Parts (Richard Schwartz) or the IFS Workbook.


7E. Propranolol-Assisted Reconsolidation
─────────────────────────────────────────

Begins: Phase 3. 4–6 sessions trialed and understood.
Prescription required: 10–20mg propranolol per session.

When a fear memory is reactivated, it temporarily becomes labile
— it can be modified before reconsolidating. Propranolol, a
beta-blocker, administered during this reconsolidation window
impairs the re-storage of the emotional component while leaving
the factual memory intact. The trigger sound is still recognized;
the threat response attached to it is weakened.

Protocol: take propranolol 60–90 minutes before a CC session.
During the wait window, run tDCS (20 min) as normal. Begin CC
session at the 90-minute mark. Work at one level above current
working SUDs — reactivation needs to reach the emotional memory
to open the reconsolidation window. tVNS throughout.

Target SUDs during propranolol sessions: 5–7. Enough activation
to open the window. Not flooding.

Log every propranolol CC session: SUDs before, peak during,
after session, and 24 hours later. The 24-hour re-rating is the
most meaningful data point — does the sound carry less charge
the following day?

Avoid: within 48 hours of macrodose or MDMA session. Not on
Red days. Not with asthma, bradycardia, or hypotension.


7F. MDMA Session 1
────────────────────

Dose: 80–100mg (first session — start lower if uncertain)
Timing: Weeks 6–8 of Phase 3
Target: the interpersonal-violation layer — specific people
whose sounds carry relational charge beyond the acoustic trigger.

MDMA produces a state of heightened empathy, reduced fear
response, and increased psychological safety. In this state,
memories and relationships that feel threatening can be
approached with curiosity rather than defense. The goal is
not to eliminate the trigger — it is to loosen the relational
charge that amplifies it.

Setting: private, safe, unhurried. A trusted sitter is strongly
recommended for MDMA sessions.

Preparation (7–10 days before): no alcohol 7 days, sleep

Hours 0–1 — Onset: lie or sit comfortably. Allow the effects
to develop. Do not rush toward therapeutic content.

Hours 1–3 — Peak: follow what arises. If specific people's
sounds arise — the ones carrying interpersonal charge — stay
with the felt sense. What is the charge actually about? What
happens to that charge when fear is temporarily removed?

introduce lowest-charge interpersonal trigger sound at low volume.
Positive music as background. 2–3 repetitions only. No tVNS
during MDMA sessions.

Hours 3–4 — Landing: journal or voice-record. What shifted.

Days 2–7: daily standalone CC — this is the Pulse.

Resume microdosing Day 4–5 after integration, once settled.

Minimum interval between MDMA sessions: 6 weeks. Do not exceed
two MDMA sessions in any 12-month period.

9A. Deliberate Re-Entry
────────────────────────

The most meaningful measure of protocol success is behavioral:
what situations are you no longer avoiding?

Phase 5 answers this through deliberate re-entry — choosing to
engage with previously avoided contexts, by choice rather than
schedule or pressure. Not exposure therapy. Choosing, from the
protocol's new baseline, to be present and let the nervous system
demonstrate what has changed.

Sequence: identify the current avoidance inventory. Begin from
the lowest-charge item. Choose to enter it without the full
protective stack. Note what is different. Progress up the list
over weeks and months as readiness arrives.

Protective tools are removed from specific contexts when the
nervous system demonstrates it no longer needs them — not when
a calendar suggests it.

SECTION 10 — REFERENCE: SOMATIC RELEASE PROTOCOL
================================================================================

PHASE 1 VERSION — SIMPLIFIED (7–10 minutes, morning only)

Begins Week 3 of Phase 1.

Constructive rest (5 min): lie on back, knees bent, feet flat,
hands on lower belly. Allow the lower back to release toward
the floor. Breathe into the hands. Jaw released, eyes soft.

Diaphragmatic reset (2–3 min): inhale 4 counts into the belly,
pause 2 counts, exhale 6 counts. Allow jaw and shoulders to
release on each exhale.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PHASE 3 ONWARD — FULL SEQUENCE (20–25 min before CC;
10–15 min morning only)

Constructive rest (5 min): on back, knees bent, feet flat,
hands on lower belly. Lower back releases toward floor.
Breathe into the hands. Jaw released. Eyes soft.

Psoas release (5 min): from constructive rest, allow one knee
to slowly drop outward — do not force. Hold 2 minutes, return.
Other side. The psoas connects to the diaphragm; its release
directly reduces the brainstem's danger signal.

Hip circles (3 min): standing, hands on hips. Slow circles
1 minute each direction, then figure-eights.

Shoulder and neck release (3 min): slow head semicircles forward
only. Shoulder shrugs held 3 seconds, released. Jaw drops,
holds 5 seconds, releases.

Diaphragmatic reset (3 min): inhale 4 counts into belly, pause
2 counts, exhale 6 counts with audible release.

Integration (2 min): standing, notice body, shake out hands,
one slow breath.

Avoid: do not force sharp pain. The psoas release should feel
like release, not stretch.

SECTION 11 — REFERENCE: YOGA NIDRA PROTOCOL
================================================================================

11A. What Yoga Nidra Is in This Protocol
──────────────────────────────────────────

Yoga Nidra is used here as thalamic filter training. In the
theta-alpha boundary state it produces (brainwaves 4–12 Hz,
just above sleep onset), the thalamus remains active as a
sensory filter while the cortex enters low arousal. Sounds
register and pass without the cortical threat cascade being
fully online.

By practicing open awareness of ambient sounds — including
trigger-range sounds — from this state, the thalamic filter
is trained directly. Sounds that would normally activate the
threat cascade begin to register as information rather than
threat.

Distinct from resonance frequency breathing (vagal regulation
from alert state) and CC (conscious association rewriting).
Yoga Nidra trains what happens to sensory input in the
passively filtered state — building intuitive awareness through
direct experience.


11B. Protocol
──────────────

Timing: every evening, before sleep. Never skipped.

Track progression:
Phases 1–2: 20-minute tracks
Phase 3: 30–35-minute tracks
Phase 4 onward: 40–45-minute tracks

Track selection: must include body scan, visualization, and
open awareness of external sounds phases. The open awareness
phase is where the thalamic filter training happens.

Starting points: Jennifer Piercy "Yoga Nidra for Sleep,"
Huberman Lab NSDR, Ally Boothroyd. Test multiple tracks and
identify 2–3 that reliably produce the hypnagogic edge —
aware but deeply relaxed, body absent or heavy, time sense
altered.


11C. Open Awareness Phase
──────────────────────────

Do not redirect attention away from ambient sounds — including
sounds from community spaces. Let them register. Label
internally if helpful: "water," "voices," "movement." Allow
them to pass without bodily reaction.

If a high-trigger sound intrudes and breaks the state: note it,
return to breathing, re-enter the body scan. The recovery —
the return to stillness after disruption — is itself the
training. It is more valuable than an undisturbed session.


11D. Night Waking
──────────────────

If waking during the night: do not check the phone. Put on the
20-minute sleep-specific Yoga Nidra track. This maintains the
theta-alpha state even if sleep does not return, preventing the
cortisol-driven frustration loop that raises the following day's
reactive baseline.

SECTION 12 — REFERENCE: QI-GONG PROTOCOL
================================================================================

Daily, 15 minutes, morning. Begins Week 4 of Phase 1.

Two components practiced in sequence.

COMPONENT 1 — SHAKING PRACTICE (3–5 minutes)

Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent.
Begin gentle, rhythmic bouncing from the knees — allowing the
whole body to vibrate softly. Let the arms hang loose and shake
with the movement. Allow the jaw to release. Breathe naturally.

This breaks overnight somatic bracing held in the joints and
fascia. The vibration is not performance — it should feel
effortless, like allowing the body to shake rather than making
it shake.

On days of significant illness: shaking only, 3 minutes.

COMPONENT 2 — SLOW FLOWING PRACTICE (10 minutes)

Slow, intentional arm and torso movement. The specific form
matters less than the quality of attention: full presence in
the body, coordinated breath, maintaining internal warmth while
external calm holds. Options include: basic Eight Brocades
(Ba Duan Jin) sequence, simple Tai Chi warm-up forms, or any
slow flowing movement sequence you return to consistently.

Exhale on exertion, inhale on opening. Allow 2–3 breath cycles
at the end before transitioning.

Continuity matters more than perfection. A consistent 15-minute
practice done imperfectly outperforms an occasional perfect
practice.

SECTION 13 — REFERENCE: COUNTERCONDITIONING TECHNIQUES
================================================================================

Six techniques in order of introduction. Each builds on the
last. Do not advance to the next technique until the previous
one is consistently producing post-session SUDs reduction.

TECHNIQUE 1 — POSITIVE PRIMING + TRIGGER INTRODUCTION
(Phases 2–5, primary technique)

1. Full somatic release sequence (see Section 10).
2. Resonance frequency breathing — 3–5 min. Confirm SUDs 0–2
   and body tension below 4/10. If not achievable: Yellow day.
3. Nuropod clipped and running 2–3 min before beginning (optional —
   use if it enhances the session without distraction).
4. Begin positive stimuli playlist. 2–3 min. Wait for felt
   positive state in the body.
5. Introduce trigger sound at low volume. Positive music dominant.
   Hold 2–4 min. Positive state must remain felt throughout.
6. Remove trigger. Return to positive stimuli. Let positive state
   rebuild fully — minimum 2 min.
7. Repeat steps 4–6 for 4–6 repetitions per session.
   Stop if: SUDs rise above 4, positive state cannot be recovered,
   or body tension rises above 5/10.
8. End on positive stimuli. Final 2 min of positive state only.
9. Note: SUDs before session, SUDs after final repetition.

TECHNIQUE 2 — GRADUATED VOLUME EXPOSURE
(Phases 2–3, once Technique 1 is running smoothly)

Same protocol as Technique 1. Add one variable: systematically
increase trigger sound volume across sessions (not within one
session). Start at the volume where SUDs remain 1–2. Increase
by the smallest perceptible step when that volume no longer
produces more than a flicker.

Volume is a hierarchy dimension. The same sound at higher volume
may be a full hierarchy level above the same sound at lower volume.

TECHNIQUE 3 — INTEROCEPTIVE AWARENESS DURING TRIGGER
(Phase 3 onward)

Modification of Technique 1. During the trigger exposure window:
instead of directing attention toward the positive stimuli,
direct it inward to body sensation. Where is the response
registering? What quality does it have? Is it hot or cold, tight
or loose, sharp or diffuse? This is not suppression — it is
curiosity. The shift from threat-scanning to interoception
during trigger exposure directly trains the competing circuit.

Do not attempt Technique 3 until Technique 1 is running well.
Maintain positive stimuli as the background; the inward attention
is a layer on top of it, not a replacement.

TECHNIQUE 4 — POSITIVE BODY ANCHOR
(Phase 3 onward, pairs with Technique 1 or 3)

Before the session, identify a specific body location that
reliably holds the positive state — often the chest, sternum,
or upper belly. During trigger exposure, keep one hand gently
resting on that location. The physical contact maintains somatic
connection to the positive state while the trigger plays.
Particularly useful when the positive state becomes hard to
hold at higher hierarchy levels.

TECHNIQUE 5 — IMAGERY + TRIGGER
(Phase 3 onward, for person-specific sounds)

For sounds whose charge is primarily relational — a specific
person's voice or eating sounds — add a compassion imagery
layer. Before introducing the trigger sound, spend 2–3 minutes
cultivating a genuine sense of warmth toward the person. Not
forgiveness (this is not a forgiveness exercise) — warmth. A
memory of connection, of something genuine between you. Then
introduce the trigger sound from that state.

This directly addresses the interpersonal-violation layer that
standard CC cannot reach. It is not available until Phase 3,
when EMDR and MDMA work have already begun loosening the
relational charge.

TECHNIQUE 6 — REAL-WORLD GRADUATED EXPOSURE
(Phase 4–5, as CC hierarchy approaches real-world levels)

The formal CC session is a laboratory. Real-world graduated
exposure moves the work into actual context. Choose one
specific real-world context from the avoidance inventory.
Enter it with: bone conduction headphones at barely perceptible
volume as background, full resonance frequency breathing
capability available, clear exit plan, Nuropod available if
appropriate. The target is a regulated experience in the real
context — not flooding. After: post-event reset (Section 5F).

Log each real-world graduated exposure session the same way
as a formal CC session: SUDs before, during, after.

PAIRED STIMULI LIBRARY — MAINTENANCE

Re-test your positive stimuli library every phase transition.
What produced genuine felt positive state in Phase 1 may have
lost its potency by Phase 3. Refresh the library by:
— Returning to live music, natural settings, or physical
  experiences that produced the strongest response
— Adding new material from recent meaningful experiences
— Removing material that has lost its felt impact

The positive stimuli library is as important as the trigger
hierarchy. A depleted positive library predicts session failure.

SOUND LIBRARY SOURCES

FOAMS database (Zenodo, DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.4813431):
32 validated trigger files across 8 sound classes. Primary
source for SUDs 1–6 range.

Freesound.org: additional sourcing for specific sound categories.
yt-dlp: extract audio from YouTube for specific real-world sounds.
Self-recording: essential for highest-hierarchy and
person-specific material.

Organize by hierarchy level. Know the SUDs rating before any
session. Never introduce a sound without knowing its rating.

SECTION 14 — REFERENCE: NUTRITION AND FOOD PROTOCOL
================================================================================

14A. Principles
────────────────

The protocol is plant-forward with fish used occasionally rather
than as a staple. The default is whole-food vegan; fish appears
when it is the practical or highest-leverage option for specific
mechanistic needs — EPA+DHA loading, phosphatidylserine on
mackerel days. Supplements carry the load when food cannot
realistically hit the target dose at protocol quantities.

The nutrition protocol has two jobs. Primary: sustain the
biological substrate neuroplasticity runs on — BDNF support,
cellular repair during fasting windows, omega-3 for neural
membrane remodeling, and gut-brain axis health. Secondary:
longevity and cellular wellness from Phase 5 onward.

These are not separate agendas. A well-functioning gut produces
neurotransmitters. A well-maintained cellular environment supports
the NAD+ metabolism that powers neuroplasticity. The brain and
the body are the same system.

The food list is organized in two tiers: what is accessible now
without cooking infrastructure, and what becomes available when
kitchen access allows. The cross-reference in Section 14E maps
food to supplements and supplements to food — what can be reduced
or retired, and which gaps the supplement stack does not currently
cover.


14B. Fasting Window
────────────────────

Target: 15–16 hours daily minimum. On aerobic/waterfall days,
extend naturally — first food at 11:30am after the waterfall
(19h+ from the previous day's 4:15pm closer).

The fasting window activates autophagy and AMPK signaling,
upregulates BDNF, and improves insulin sensitivity. These are
not abstract benefits — they directly affect the efficiency of
every CC session.

Practical anchors:
— Resistance training days: Meal 1 is the post-training protein
  smoothie (~6:30am) with the full supplement stack. Meal 2 at
  11:30am (light). Main Meal at 2:45pm. Last calories 4:15pm.
— Aerobic/waterfall days: meeting fasted, waterfall fasted,
  first food at 11:30am post-waterfall. Main Meal at 2:45pm.
  Last calories 4:15pm. Fasting window reaches 19h+.

ACV (1 tbsp) immediately before the 2:45pm Main Meal — the
carb-heaviest meal of the day, not on an empty stomach.


14C. Core Foods — Daily Baseline
──────────────────────────────────
(Accessible without cooking infrastructure.)

OMEGA-3 AND NEURAL MEMBRANE SUPPORT

Oily fish — sardines, mackerel, salmon (occasional, 1–2x per
week when practical):
~1500–2000mg EPA+DHA per 100g. Fish is the exception, not the
anchor — algae oil carries this load daily. When fish is eaten:
skip krill oil and algae oil supplements. On mackerel specifically:
skip or halve phosphatidylserine (~480mg PS per 100g mackerel).
See Section 14F.

Walnuts — 30g daily:
ALA (~2.5g per 30g), ellagitannins, polyphenols. Ellagitannins
are converted by gut bacteria to urolithin A — a mitochondrial
renewal compound on a pathway distinct from NMN and NAD+. Regular
walnut consumption supports this pathway continuously. Also
provides conversion substrate for the omega-3 stack on non-fish
days.

Ground flaxseed — 1–2 tbsp daily:
ALA, lignans with anti-inflammatory properties, soluble fiber.
Dissolves in smoothies. Combined with walnuts, ALA substrate is
consistent on non-fish days.

BDNF AND NEUROPLASTICITY

Blueberries or blackberries — 100–150g daily:
Pterostilbene and anthocyanins upregulate BDNF through a pathway
similar to resveratrol. Before Phase 5's resveratrol supplement
begins, berries prime the same sirtuin-pathway mechanism. After
it begins, they remain complementary. Frozen is equivalent to
fresh for this purpose.

CELLULAR RENEWAL AND LONGEVITY

Wheat germ — 1–2 tbsp daily:
Highest spermidine concentration per gram of any food. Triggers
autophagy independently of the fasting window. Add to smoothies
or oats.

Pomegranate arils or concentrate — 80–100g arils or 30ml
concentrate daily:
Ellagitannins → urolithin A via gut bacteria. This mitochondrial
cleanup pathway is not covered by any supplement in the stack.
Pomegranate concentrate in the mini fridge allows daily use
without preparation.

Capers — daily, add to anything:
Highest quercetin food source (~20mg per tablespoon). Senolytic.

Strawberries — weekly:
Fisetin. Senolytic with the most direct clinical evidence.

Dark chocolate 85%+ — 20–30g daily:
Flavanols and epicatechin improve cerebrovascular function and
BDNF expression. Also provides ~64mg elemental magnesium per 30g,
reducing the nightly Mg Glycinate supplemental requirement.
Theobromine adds mild methylxanthine support.

GUT-BRAIN AXIS

L. reuteri (supplement) handles the oxytocin/vagal signaling
mechanism. The fermented foods below diversify the broader
microbiome that the supplement alone cannot cover — GABA
production, serotonin precursor environment, and short-chain
fatty acid support for neuroinflammation baseline.

Fermented foods — rotate daily, one or more from: natto, kimchi,
sauerkraut, miso.
Different fermented foods feed different bacterial populations.
Rotating rather than defaulting to one source maximizes
microbiome diversity.

Natto — 10g daily:
The most mechanistically efficient single food addition in this
protocol. 10g natto = ~100mcg K2 MK-7 — exactly the supplement
dose. Skip the K2 MK-7 supplement on natto days. Natto also
contributes spermidine and Bacillus subtilis var. natto. Store
frozen; thaw overnight in the mini fridge. 10g is small enough
to stir invisibly into rice or miso soup.

VITAMINS, MINERALS, AND PROTOCOL GAPS

Nutritional yeast — 2 tbsp daily:
B-complex broadly (B1, B2, B3, B5, B6, folate) and fortified
B12. Reduces — but does not replace — the B-complex supplement
gap; methylated forms (methylfolate, methylcobalamin) remain
supplement-dependent. Add to soups, dressings, or smoothies.

Soy or sunflower lecithin granules — 1 tbsp daily:
Choline (~375mg as phosphatidylcholine). The most significant
micronutrient gap in the current supplement stack on a plant-
forward protocol. Choline is essential for acetylcholine synthesis,
neuronal membrane integrity, and one-carbon methylation. A
plant-forward diet without deliberate choline sourcing is
reliably deficient at the levels this protocol demands. The
granules dissolve in a smoothie. Take with or before the first
meal.

Pumpkin seeds (pepitas) — 30g daily:
~150mg elemental magnesium (reduces Mg Glycinate supplement
requirement) and ~7mg zinc. Zinc is absent from the supplement
stack and is essential for BDNF synthesis, testosterone, and
glutathione peroxidase activity alongside NAC. Most practical
vegan zinc source in this protocol.

Brazil nuts — 2–3 nuts daily (not more):
100–200mcg selenium per 2–3 nuts. Selenium is absent from the
supplement stack and essential for glutathione peroxidase — the
enzyme NAC ultimately serves — thyroid T4→T3 conversion, and
neuroprotection. Do not exceed 3 nuts. Upper tolerable intake is
400mcg/day; selenium toxicity has a narrow margin.

Tart cherry concentrate — 1 tbsp in the evening wind-down:
The only significant dietary melatonin source. Used once the
melatonin supplement is discontinued (Weeks 4–6), tart cherry
maintains the sleep onset mechanism from a food source. Also
provides anthocyanins and quercetin, and reduces exercise-induced
inflammation — relevant alongside resistance training. Add to
warm water as part of the bedtime sequence.

Apple cider vinegar — 1 tbsp before meals:
AMPK activation, blood sugar management.

Spice base daily — turmeric (with black pepper + fat), ginger,
oregano, garlic: anti-inflammatory, NF-kB modulation, AMPK.


14D. Core Foods — Kitchen Access

(Require hotplate or community kitchen. Prioritize when available.)

When harvested from the grow station: sauté briefly in oil.

Highest dietary nitrate concentration alongside arugula. The
nitrate → nitrite → nitric oxide pathway produces vasodilatory
and cerebrovascular effects within 2–3 hours. Improved cerebral
blood flow from dietary nitrates is documented in acute cognitive
performance trials. Roast in bulk; keeps 4–5 days refrigerated.
Particularly relevant on training days and before intensive
cognitive work blocks.

Arugula (rocket) — daily when kitchen access allows:
Highest nitrate concentration of any common leafy green (~480mg
per 100g). Combined with beets 2–3x per week, the NO pathway

as a raw salad without full kitchen use. The beet-arugula
combination supports the same cerebrovascular effects the
waterfall run and cold immersion activate through exercise.

Leafy greens broadly — daily:
Folate (reduces B-complex supplement gap), lutein, K1, nitrates.
Spinach cooked (157mg magnesium per cup) meaningfully reduces the
magnesium supplement load alongside pumpkin seeds and dark
chocolate.

Broccoli sprouts or broccoli — 3–4x per week:
Sulforaphane. Raw sprouts require no cooking and are accessible
now. For broccoli: chop 40 minutes before cooking; add mustard
seed powder to cooked broccoli to restore sulforaphane yield.

Artichokes — weekly:
One artichoke head provides ~70mg apigenin — above the 50mg
supplement dose. Skip the apigenin supplement on artichoke days.
Also provides cynarin (liver support, relevant with the supplement
load) and inulin (prebiotic fiber for the gut-brain axis). Steam
whole: 30–40 minutes, minimal attention required.

Celery seed — 1 tsp (7g) in cooking:
~50mg apigenin per 7g — the most efficient cooking-level
apigenin source. Add to dressings, soups, or spice mixes. Skip
the apigenin supplement on days celery seed is in rotation.
When used consistently, the apigenin supplement becomes redundant.

Tempeh — 2–3x per week:
Spermidine (complements wheat germ), complete protein, fermented
probiotic culture distinct from kimchi and sauerkraut. Sauté or
crumble into bowls.

Legumes — daily or near-daily:
Lentils (179mcg folate per 100g cooked — highest plant source),
white beans (107mg PS per 100g), chickpeas, black beans.
Combined folate from daily legumes and leafy greens significantly
reduces the gap the B-complex supplement fills. White beans
(2 cups, ~214mg PS) partially cover the phosphatidylserine
supplement on non-fish days.

Edamame — regularly:
Complete protein, choline (~63mg per 100g, complements lecithin
granules), trace NMN contribution.

Oyster mushrooms — daily when available:
Ergothioneine — the longevity antioxidant most strongly linked
to reduced disease risk in large-scale blood analysis. The grow
station produces these. Heat-stable; powder form also works.

PART 2 — FOODS FILLING GAPS THE SUPPLEMENT STACK DOESN'T COVER

The following address mechanisms not currently covered by any
supplement in the protocol:

Lecithin granules (1 tbsp): Choline ~375mg as phosphatidyl-
choline. Acetylcholine synthesis, neuronal membrane integrity,
methylation. The most significant micronutrient gap for a
plant-forward protocol — not in the supplement stack.

Brazil nuts (2–3): Selenium ~100–200mcg. Glutathione peroxidase
activity (the system NAC serves), thyroid T4→T3 conversion,
neuroprotection. Not currently supplemented.

Beets + arugula: Dietary nitrates → nitric oxide. Cerebrovascular
dilation, brain oxygenation, training performance. The NO pathway
exercise and cold immersion activate is supported from below by
dietary nitrates through a separate mechanism.

Pomegranate + walnuts: Ellagitannins → urolithin A. Mitophagy.
Distinct from and complementary to the NMN/NAD+ longevity stack.
Not available as a meaningful supplement equivalent.

Tart cherry: Melatonin (post-taper maintenance), anthocyanins,
exercise recovery support.

Blueberries/blackberries: Pterostilbene → BDNF, sirtuin primer
before Phase 5 resveratrol begins; complementary after.

Fermented rotation (natto, kimchi, sauerkraut, miso): Microbiome
diversity beyond L. reuteri — GABA substrate, serotonin precursor
environment, short-chain fatty acids for neuroinflammation.

Pumpkin seeds: Zinc ~7mg daily. Not currently supplemented.
Essential for BDNF synthesis, testosterone, glutathione
peroxidase alongside NAC.


14F. Key Interactions
──────────────────────

Fish days: skip krill oil and algae oil supplements.
Mackerel days: also skip or halve phosphatidylserine
  (~480mg PS per 100g mackerel covers the 300mg supplement dose).
Natto days: skip K2 MK-7 supplement. D3 still taken as normal.
Artichoke (1 head) or celery seed (7g) in cooking: skip apigenin
  supplement that day.
UV-exposed shiitake (100g): reduce D3 supplement by 1,000–2,000 IU.
Fresh lion's mane harvest (100–150g sautéed): halve Lion's Mane
  supplement dose that day.
Green tea and iron: space 1 hour either side of iron-rich meals.
Berberine: not on resistance training days.
NMN: empty stomach or before other morning food for sublingual
  absorption.
D3/K2: with fat-containing food. On natto days, D3 is still taken.
Magnesium: at bedtime. Pumpkin seeds and dark chocolate can be
  consumed at any time.
Brazil nuts: 2–3 per day maximum. Selenium toxicity threshold
  applies above 3 nuts (upper tolerable intake 400mcg/day).
Lecithin granules: with or before first meal.
Pomegranate concentrate: no known interactions with protocol
  supplements.
Tart cherry: take in the evening wind-down. Supports sleep
  onset — does not interfere with it.

SECTION 15 — REFERENCE: MEASUREMENT AND TRACKING
================================================================================

Tracking serves the protocol, not the other way around. The
primary instrument is felt sense. Data is a secondary
confirmation tool. Any tracking that feels like a burden
should be simplified or dropped.


15A. Weekly (Sunday, 10 minutes)
─────────────────────────────────

Trigger hierarchy re-rating: re-rate the full hierarchy. The
shift from baseline to current is the most direct measure of
acoustic-sensory layer progress.

Felt-sense note: one sentence capturing the quality of the
week's nervous system state. Not analysis — just record.

CC session note: after each CC session (in the moment, not
Sunday) — 2 minutes: SUDs before and after, one observation.


15B. Monthly
─────────────

A-MISO-S questionnaire: 5 minutes. Track monthly from Phase 0
baseline. A 25% reduction (from 18 to ~13–14) is the Phase 2
benchmark.

HRV trend review: one glance at the monthly summary. Trend up,
flat, or down. Done.


15C. Quarterly
───────────────

Avoidance inventory: list every situation still being avoided
or heavily managed. The shrinking of this list over quarters
is the most meaningful functional outcome measure.


15D. Biological Testing Schedule
──────────────────────────────────

Phase 0: full bloodwork, salivary cortisol, NAD+ blood level.

Phase 4 (if logistics allow): repeat cortisol and bloodwork.

Year 1 completion: full bloodwork, salivary cortisol, NAD+,
Omega-3 Index (OmegaQuant — mail-in, no physician required),
epigenetic age test (TruAge or Elysium Index).

Year 2+: full panel annually.

Supplement washout before draws:
2 weeks before: Lugol's iodine (if used), high-dose biotin.
48–72 hours before: creatine, zinc, magnesium forms, NMN.
Morning of draw: D3/K2, Ashwagandha, resveratrol as normal.
No change: B12, krill, omega-3 top-up, Bacopa, Lion's Mane,
  NAC, L. reuteri.

LEGAL AND SAFETY NOTE
================================================================================

Psilocybin and MDMA are Schedule I substances in the United
States. Hawaii has no state-level decriminalization. Know your
legal context before proceeding.

Hard contraindications for psilocybin and MDMA: personal or
family history of psychosis, schizophrenia, or bipolar I;
current SSRIs, SNRIs, pharmaceutical lithium, or MAOIs; heart
conditions; pregnancy.

Stellate Ganglion Block requires an experienced physician and
full medical history review. Insist on ultrasound guidance.

Nothing in this document is medical advice.
